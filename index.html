Proposed workshop at FSE 2027

AgenticWeb3 2027

The 1st International Workshop on Agentic Web3

Proposal stage. This is a proposal for a workshop at FSE 2027. Workshop acceptance is not yet confirmed, and the dates, program, and committee are provisional. Submissions are not yet open.

Agentic Web3 integrates autonomous AI agents with decentralized software and infrastructure. The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners to advance software engineering across two complementary directions.

Agents for Web3
Using agents to develop, test, analyze, maintain, and operate smart contracts and decentralized applications.
Web3 for Agents
Using decentralized mechanisms to support agent identity, authorization, coordination, trust, and economic interactions.

Both directions raise engineering questions across the software lifecycle. We aim to identify requirements and failure modes, compare methods and deployment evidence, and encourage reproducible evaluation and collaboration between academia and industry.

Topics of interest

We welcome contributions across both directions, from construction and integration to assurance, operation, and evaluation. Each submission should identify its concrete connection to agents and Web3. Topics include, but are not limited to, the following.

Requirements and construction

  • Requirements engineering for agent and Web3 interactions.
  • Agent-assisted development of contracts and Web3 applications.
  • Planning and synthesis of agent workflows using Web3 services.
  • Human oversight and approval of on-chain agent actions.

Architecture and interoperability

  • Architectures for agents and decentralized infrastructure.
  • Agent framework, protocol, wallet, and tool interoperability.
  • Coordination across agents, chains, and off-chain services.
  • Decentralized compute and storage for agent workloads.

Identity, authorization, and trust

  • Decentralized agent identity, discovery, and authentication.
  • Authorization and delegation in agent workflows.
  • Trust and reputation mechanisms for agent collaboration.
  • Verifiable computation and attestation for agent services.

Economic coordination and governance

  • Contracting and incentive mechanisms in agent service markets.
  • Autonomous payments and resource budgeting for agents.
  • Service delivery verification, settlement, and dispute resolution.
  • Agent participation in decentralized governance.

Security and privacy

  • Security of agent inputs, tools, and software supply chains.
  • Wallet and key security in Web3 systems operated by agents.
  • Secure communication and transactions among agents.
  • Privacy-preserving agent collaboration and accountability.
  • Provenance and integrity of agent models and data.

Testing and experimentation

  • Agent-assisted testing of contracts and blockchain systems.
  • Testing and fuzzing of integrated agent and Web3 systems.
  • Test oracles for nondeterministic and stateful agent behavior.
  • Fault injection and reproducible experiments for agent workflows.

Analysis and verification

  • Agent-assisted program analysis and smart-contract auditing.
  • Static and dynamic analysis of Agentic Web3 systems.
  • Security analysis of information and authority flows.
  • Verifying safety and liveness across agents and contracts.
  • Validation of agent-generated Web3 software and actions.

Runtime monitoring and recovery

  • Runtime policy enforcement for autonomous Web3 actions.
  • Observability and causal tracing of distributed agent execution.
  • Failure attribution and accountability in agent workflows.
  • Fault tolerance and recovery for partially completed workflows.

Maintenance and evolution

  • Agent-assisted debugging and repair of decentralized software.
  • Compatibility and regressions after protocol or API updates.
  • Migration and evolution of integrated agent and Web3 systems.
  • Lifecycle management of agent tools and dependencies.

Evaluation and human interaction

  • Benchmarks and empirical studies of Agentic Web3 systems.
  • Performance, cost, and scalability of agent workflows.
  • Comparing decentralized, centralized, and federated designs.
  • Usability and user understanding of Agentic Web3 systems.

Submission information

The proposed workshop welcomes research contributions and tool demonstrations from academia and industry. Submission instructions and the submission link will be announced after workshop acceptance.

2–5 pages including references and appendicesExtended abstracts for both contribution typesSingle-anonymous review

Research contributions

Submissions should explain a concrete engineering problem, its connection to Agentic Web3, the proposed approach, and evidence supporting the claims. Evaluation will consider relevance, methodological appropriateness, clarity, and potential to stimulate discussion.

Tool demonstrations

Describe the engineering problem, key capabilities, current implementation, and a concrete demonstration scenario. Links to an accessible artifact or short video are encouraged. Review will also consider practical usefulness, evidence of a working implementation, and the feasibility of the demonstration.

Review and planned publication

Each submission will receive at least three conflict-free PC reviews. Research contributions and tool demonstrations will share one archival category: extended abstracts. Subject to workshop acceptance, the publication plan is to include accepted contributions in the ACM Digital Library as extended abstracts, which the FSE 2027 workshop call identifies as exempt from article processing charges.

Presentation and participation

At least one author of each accepted contribution must register and present in person. Tool demonstration authors will also demonstrate their tools. Attendance will be open to registered participants without an accepted contribution.

Submission eligibility and conflicts will follow SIGSOFT policy. Organizers and their directly supervised students, postdoctoral researchers, and employees may not submit. The final call for papers will include the required ACM publication-policy notice, including the policy on research involving human participants.

Planned program

A one-day, in-person workshop with invited talks, contributed presentations, and academic and industrial tool demonstrations. The detailed schedule and speakers will be announced after acceptance.

  1. Opening and invited talks

    An introduction to the workshop and one or two invited talks on research challenges and practical experience.

  2. Agents for Web3

    Research on developing, testing, and operating decentralized software with agents.

  3. Web3 for Agents

    Research on decentralized support for agent collaboration and economic interaction.

  4. Demonstration session

    Working tools from academia and industry, with concrete workflows and discussion of capabilities, limitations, and practical use.

Questions and discussion will be integrated throughout the sessions to connect findings and identify open problems.
